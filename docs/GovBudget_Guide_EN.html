1. About this system

GovBudget replaces input-based budgeting with performance-based budgeting (PBB).

Traditional budgeting asks what will we spend money on? — salaries, supplies, equipment. Performance-based budgeting asks what will we deliver, and what does delivering it cost? The money is the same; the question is different, and so is the answer you can give.

GovBudget answers the second question by attaching every cost to the activity it pays for. Three streams of cost converge on activities:

  • HR — each employee's annual cost, spread across the activities they work on.
  • OPEX — operating expenditure, entered against an activity.
  • CAPEX — capital expenditure, entered against an activity or project.

Once every dirham sits on an activity, the system can roll costs up into programmes, redistribute overhead onto the work it supports, and report cost per output. That chain — employee to activity to programme to output — is what this guide walks through.

What this guide replaces This document supersedes the separate User Guide, User Manual, Administrator Guide, Submission Workflow Guide and What-If Guide. Everything they covered is here, brought up to date with the current system.

2. The budgeting model — concepts you need first

Five terms carry most of the meaning in GovBudget. Reading the rest of this guide is much easier once these are clear.

Entity and cost centre

An entity is a government organisation or authority — for example the Customs Department or the Department of Antiquities and Museums. A cost centre (also called a department) is a unit inside an entity. Your account is normally tied to one entity, and everything you see is limited to it.

Programme

A programme is a block of work an entity delivers. Every programme is classified as one of two types, and the classification matters a great deal:

TypeMeaning
Mandate Core mission work — the reason the entity exists. Excavating a site, clearing goods at a border, running a museum. Mandate programmes receive allocated overhead.
Support Overhead that enables the mandate work — finance, HR, IT, general administration. Support programme cost can be pushed out onto the mandate activities it supports.

Activity

An activity is specific work inside a programme, and it is where costs are actually entered. If you remember one thing from this section, make it that: budget lines attach to activities, not to programmes. Programme figures are always the sum of their activities.

Project

A project is a defined, usually time-bound piece of work — typically capital. Costs can be tagged to a project as well as an activity, which lets you report by project without breaking the activity structure.

Direct cost and total cost

This distinction appears throughout the reports, and misreading it is the single most common source of confusion.

BasisWhat it shows
Direct Every cost stays exactly where it was entered. This is the default and it is always available. A support programme shows its own cost.
Total
fully loaded		 The latest posted allocation run is overlaid: support programme cost is added to the mandate programmes it supports (allocated-in) and removed from the support programme (allocated-out). A support programme therefore nets to zero.
A support programme showing zero is correct, not a fault On the Total basis, a support programme has had all of its cost moved onto the mandate work it supports. Showing it in both places would double-count the budget. To see the support programme's own cost, switch the report to the Direct basis.

3. Getting started: signing in, context and navigation

Signing in

  1. Open the application address in your browser.
  2. Enter your username and password and choose Sign in.
  3. If this is your first sign-in, or an administrator has reset your password, you are asked to choose a new one before you can continue.
Account lockout Five consecutive wrong passwords lock the account for fifteen minutes. Wait, or ask an administrator to unlock it and issue a reset link — you do not need to create a new account.

Choosing your context

Almost every screen works within a context: a budget year, an entity and a cost centre. Set it once from Budget Setup and the system remembers it for your session.

If a screen looks empty or refuses to save, the context is the first thing to check — you may be looking at a different year or cost centre than you think.

4. Roles and what each one can do

There are four roles. Which one you hold determines both what appears in your menu and what you can do on each screen.

RoleScope
SYSADMIN Full access to everything, across all entities. Deliberately unrestricted so that a mistake in the permissions screen can never lock everyone out.
Global ADMIN An administrator with no entity assigned. Sees and administers every entity. Everything except the Roles & Rights screen.
Entity ADMIN An administrator with an entity assigned. Full administration inside that entity only, and blocked from the cross-entity tools — the Cost Allocation engine and the Management Review pack.
USER Prepares budgets within an assigned entity and cost centre. Can submit for approval but not approve. Read access to reports.
Global versus entity administrator The difference is simply whether the account has an entity attached. An administrator with no entity is global; attaching one immediately narrows them to it. This is set on the user record — see Administration.

A VIEWER role also exists for review-only access: it can see the budget, reports and performance screens but change nothing.

5. Preparing a budget

Applies to USER Entity ADMIN Global ADMIN SYSADMIN

5.1 Budget Setup

Start here. Choose the year, entity and cost centre you are budgeting for. A USER normally has only one choice available; administrators can switch.

The four budget categories are tabs across the top of the entry screen:

CategoryContents
REVENUEIncome the entity expects to collect.
HREmployee costs — imported centrally, then allocated to activities (see 5.3).
CAPEXCapital expenditure.
OPEXOperating expenditure.
Each category is approved separately REVENUE, HR, CAPEX and OPEX each have their own submission and their own status. Approving OPEX has no effect on CAPEX. This lets a settled category move forward while another is still in discussion.

5.2 Budget Entry — Revenue, OPEX and CAPEX

  1. Open Budget Setup and choose the category tab you want to work on.
  2. Add a line. You choose the activity the cost belongs to, the GL account, and where relevant the item and project.
  3. Enter quantity and unit price, or the amount directly, depending on how the line is set up.
  4. Distribute the annual amount across the twelve months if you need a monthly profile. This monthly distribution is what drives cash-basis reporting.
  5. Save. The line is now part of the current draft.
If Save is disabled or you see a "locked" message Budget lines can only be added, changed or deleted while the latest version of that category is in Draft. Any other status locks entry. See section 6 for how to get an editable draft back.

5.3 HR cost allocation

HR works differently from the other categories. You do not type salary figures. Each employee's annual cost is imported by an administrator, and the budget work is deciding which activities that person's cost belongs to.

  1. Open Budget Setup and choose the HR tab. You see the employees in your cost centre with their annual cost.
  2. Choose Allocate on an employee.
  3. Add an allocation: pick the activity (and project, if relevant) and give either a percentage of the employee's cost or an amount.
  4. Repeat until the employee is fully allocated. The screen shows allocated and remaining amounts as you go.
Aim for 100% The system prevents you allocating more than an employee's annual cost, but it does not force you to allocate all of it. Anything unallocated is real salary cost attached to no activity, so it disappears from activity and programme reporting. The Allocation Variances screen lists everyone who is not fully allocated — check it before you submit.

6. Submission and approval workflow

A submission is one category, for one year, entity and cost centre. It moves through a fixed sequence of statuses.

The lifecycle

Draft ──Submit──▶ Submitted ──Entity approve──▶ EntityApproved ──Send to central──▶ SentToCentral ──Approve──▶ SysApproved ▲ │ │ │ │ └── Return / Unlock ─────┴──────────────────────────┘ │ │ └────── a Return always creates a NEW Draft (next version); the old version is kept as history

What each status means

StatusMeaningEditable?
DraftWork in progress. Lines are being entered.Yes
SubmittedSent for approval by the budget preparer; awaiting entity review.No
EntityApprovedApproved at entity level; ready to go to the central department.No
SentToCentralWith the central finance department for final review.No
SysApprovedFinally approved. This is the settled budget.No
ReturnedSent back for changes. A new editable draft is created alongside it.No — but its successor draft is

Versions, and why nothing is ever overwritten

Every submission stream keeps numbered versions. When work is returned or unlocked, the system does not reopen the locked version. It creates a fresh Draft as the next version number and leaves the earlier one intact, marked Returned.

This matters for two reasons. You can always see what was submitted at each stage and compare versions to show exactly what changed; and an approved budget can never be quietly edited after the fact.

Step by step — budget preparer

  1. Enter your lines while the category is in Draft.
  2. Check the totals, and for HR check the Allocation Variances screen.
  3. Choose Submit on the category. It locks and moves to Submitted.
  4. If it comes back as Returned, read the return note, open the new draft that was created for you, make the changes and submit again.

Step by step — administrator

  1. Open Management Review and filter to the submissions awaiting you.
  2. Review the figures, using the version comparison if this is a resubmission.
  3. Approve to move it forward, or Return with a note explaining what needs to change.
  4. Approved entity submissions are then sent to central for final approval.
Returning is not destructive A return never deletes anyone's work. It preserves the submitted version as history and hands the preparer a fresh copy to edit. Use it freely rather than asking someone to rebuild a budget.

7. Mid-year forecast

Half way through the year, entities record what has actually been spent in the first six months and forecast the second half. Mid-Year Forecast captures both against the same GL and activity structure as the budget, so the comparison is like for like.

The result feeds the variance reporting: budget for the half year, actual for the half year, and the variance between them, alongside a revised full-year expectation.

8. Actuals and Budget vs Actual

Import: Global ADMIN Entity ADMIN · Reporting: all roles

Actual expenditure is imported rather than typed. There are two streams:

SourceGrainWhy it matters
GL / MM actuals Account and period General expenditure as posted in the finance system.
HR actuals Employee and month Per-employee payroll cost. Because it is per employee, it can be attributed to activities using the same allocation percentages as the budget — giving actual cost by activity, not just by account.

Budget vs Actual then compares the two on the same structure. Where an imported employee code does not match a budgeted employee, the amount still counts toward HR totals but cannot be attributed to an activity; the import reports how many rows were affected.

9. The cost allocation engine

SYSADMIN Global ADMIN only

This is the step that turns direct cost into fully loaded cost. It takes the cost sitting on support programmes and pushes it onto the mandate activities that support enables, using a driver such as headcount.

How to run one

  1. Open Admin Room → Cost Allocation and select the budget year.
  2. Confirm the programme classification — which programmes are Support, and the sequence in which they step down.
  3. Define the drivers (for example headcount) and enter their values per target.
  4. Create rules mapping each support programme to its target mandate activities through a driver.
  5. Run the allocation and review the resulting transactions.
  6. Post the run. Reports on the Total basis then use it.
Two guarantees worth knowing

Your original budget is never modified. Allocation results are written to their own tables. Budget lines and HR allocations are left exactly as entered — the Direct basis always shows what you actually budgeted.

Every posting nets to zero. Each transaction credits the target activity (allocated-in) and debits the source programme (allocated-out) by the same amount, so total cost across the entity is unchanged. Cost moves; it is never created or destroyed.

Runs are versioned. Posting a new run supersedes the previous one, which is retained. Reports on the Total basis always use the latest posted run; if none is posted, they fall back to Direct.

10. Employee cost per hour New

Cost per hour expresses an employee's annual cost as the cost of one hour of their work. It is used for costing services and outputs — for example, what an inspection or a permit actually costs when the staff time it consumes is priced properly.

The two rates, and which to use

Annual leave and public holidays are paid. Their cost is already inside the employee's annual cost. That leaves a choice of divisor, and the choice matters:

RateDivided byUse it for
Standard rate
the costing rate		 Productive hours — contracted hours less paid public holidays and annual leave Costing services, outputs and KPIs. This is the one to use.
Nominal rate Contracted hours — the full paid year Reference and comparison only.

The standard rate is higher, and that is the point. Dividing by contracted hours spreads salary over hours that include paid absence, so multiplying activity hours back by that rate recovers only part of the salary — the paid leave becomes cost that belongs to activities but appears nowhere. Dividing by productive hours absorbs the paid leave into the rate, so hours × rate reconciles back to the full annual cost. Both rates are shown side by side so the treatment is visible rather than hidden in a formula.

The work calendar

SYSADMIN Global ADMIN Entity ADMIN (own entity)

Hours come from a work calendar, maintained under Admin Room → Work Calendars. It is a short list of numbers:

SettingMeaning
Hours per day / Work days per week / Weeks per yearThe contracted pattern. Multiplied together they give gross paid hours.
Public holidays (days)Paid days not worked.
Annual leave (days)Paid leave entitlement.
Other paid absence (days)Paid sick or training days, if you track them. Leave at 0 otherwise.
Utilisation %Leave at 100. Lowering it breaks the reconciliation back to total salary cost.

One calendar marked All entities serves as the default. An entity only needs its own row if its working pattern genuinely differs. The screen shows how many employees each calendar drives, so you can see the effect of a change before making it.

Changes take effect immediately The rate is calculated when the report is read, not stored. Edit a calendar and the cost per hour report is correct on the next page load — there is nothing to recalculate and no deployment needed.

Reading the report

Reports → Employee Cost per Hour shows every employee with the full hour build-up — gross paid hours, holiday, leave, productive hours — and both rates, so any figure can be traced.

The summary shows a blended rate: total cost divided by total productive hours. That is deliberately not the average of the individual rates, which would weight a cleaner the same as a director and understate what an organisational hour costs.

Two kinds of row are flagged and excluded from the blended rate:

  • Vacant — a budgeted but unfilled post. Its cost is usually part-year, so an hourly rate would be misleading.
  • No calendar — no work calendar exists for that year, so no rate could be produced. Add one to bring these employees in.

Where an individual genuinely does not follow the entity calendar — a part-timer, a shift worker, a mid-year joiner — their hours can be overridden individually without disturbing anyone else.

Choosing a chart

  • Bar — comparing values across a handful of categories.
  • Bar (horizontal) — the same comparison when the names are long. Labels run down the side where there is room for them, instead of being tilted along the bottom.
  • Waterfall — how each row builds toward the total, closing with a TOTAL bar. Contributions rise in green, reductions step down in red. This is the clearest way to show how individual programmes make up an entity's budget.
  • Pie — proportions of a whole, when there are only a few slices.
  • Line — movement across an ordered dimension such as month.
Chart labels Vertical charts label the axis with the reference code alone, because a full code-and-name label is long enough to crowd out the chart itself. Hover any bar to see the full name. Horizontal bars have room for the name and show it.

A configuration you expect to reuse can be saved by name and reopened later. Saved reports remember their cost basis as well — worth knowing if a saved report keeps returning on the Total basis when you expected Direct.

11.3 Executive deck

The executive deck is a presentation, populated with real figures for the selected year and entity, that contrasts traditional input budgeting with the performance-based view. It opens in a new tab and is intended for briefing management rather than day-to-day analysis.

11.4 Printing and exporting

  • Download Excel exports the report you are looking at, with the transaction detail on additional sheets where relevant.
  • Print is set up for these reports: it defaults to landscape, drops the menus, uses the full page width, and repeats the column headers at the top of every page so a long report stays readable past page one.

12. Performance data — KPIs, outputs and maturity

Cost is only half of performance-based budgeting; the other half is what the money produced.

  • KPIs — indicators with targets and actuals, classified and linked to the activities that deliver them. Where a KPI is linked to cost, the system can express cost per unit of result.
  • Activity outputs — the countable things an activity produces (permits issued, visitors received). Combined with activity cost these give unit cost per output.
  • Maturity assessments — a structured self-assessment of how far the entity has progressed in performance budgeting practice.

Output and KPI data is entered by the entity. The quality of unit-cost reporting depends directly on it, so it is worth keeping current.

13. Management review

SYSADMIN Global ADMIN only

Management Review is where submissions are approved or returned, and it also carries the management reporting pack:

  • Cost structure and capital discipline.
  • Manpower cost per FTE, measured against a reference band.
  • Programme cost, direct and after allocation, with scenarios side by side.
  • Activity cost after allocation, and cost per output.
  • KPI scorecard and the maturity ladder.
  • An entity profile combining headline figures with review narrative.

The whole pack exports to Excel as a multi-sheet workbook.

14. What-If scenarios

What-If answers questions like what happens if the budget is cut by 10%? without touching the real budget. You create a named scenario, apply adjustments, and view reports through it.

When a scenario is active, a clear What-If Active badge appears on the reports so nobody mistakes modelled figures for the real budget. Deactivate the scenario to return to normal reporting. Scenarios never modify budget lines.

15. Requests and internal messages

Requests is a simple message channel inside the system. A budget user can raise a question or ask for something to be unlocked, and an administrator can respond, without the conversation moving to email where it is detached from the budget it concerns.

16. The budget assistant

The assistant is a chat panel that answers questions about your own budget and performance data, and about OECD performance-budgeting practice. It is read-only — it can explain and summarise figures but cannot change anything — and it answers only within the data your account is allowed to see.

It may be switched off The assistant can be disabled by configuration, for example where the hosting environment does not permit external services. When it is off, the panel reports that it is not configured. Nothing else in the system depends on it.

17. Administration

SYSADMIN Global ADMIN Entity ADMIN (own entity)

Everything administrative lives in the Admin Room.

Set-up order

Reference data has dependencies. Setting it up in this order avoids rework:

  1. Entities — the organisations.
  2. Cost Centres — units within each entity.
  3. GL Accounts — the chart of accounts, typed as revenue, OPEX or CAPEX.
  4. Items — mapped to GL accounts, for detailed entry.
  5. Programmes — with their Mandate/Support classification and allocation sequence.
  6. Activities — under programmes and mapped to cost centres. Costs land here.
  7. Projects — where project-level reporting is needed.
  8. Work Calendars — if cost per hour will be used.
  9. Users — once the structure exists to assign them to.

Users

Create the account, then assign its role, entity and cost centre. Remember that an administrator with no entity is a global administrator; assigning an entity narrows them to it.

Administrators can reset a password by issuing a reset link, and unlock an account locked by failed sign-in attempts.

Roles & Rights

SYSADMIN only

Every screen in the application is listed here, and each role can be granted view, add, edit and delete rights on it. Read-only screens such as reports only offer view.

Changes here take effect for users on their next page load. SYSADMIN always retains full access regardless of what is set, which is the safeguard that prevents an accidental save from locking everybody out of the system.

HR Costs

Employee annual costs are imported from Excel. Download the template, fill it in, and import. The importer validates entity, cost centre and GL account, reports any row it could not accept, and leaves the rest of the file to load. Existing employees for the same year are updated rather than duplicated.

Actuals

GL/MM and HR actuals are imported here — see section 8.

Audit log

Key actions are recorded with the user, timestamp and details: user administration, allocations, imports, approvals and calendar changes. Entity administrators see their own entity's entries.

Appendix C — Troubleshooting

I cannot find a programme in the dropdown.

Programmes are filtered to your entity and must be active. Check your context is on the right entity and year, and ask an administrator whether the programme exists and is active.

Save is disabled, or I see a "locked" message.

That category is not in Draft. Once submitted it is locked. Ask an administrator to return or unlock it — you will get a fresh draft to edit, and your previous submission is kept as history.

I do not see the admin menus.

They are limited to administrator roles. If you should have access, ask a system administrator to check your role and entity assignment.

A programme shows zero on a report.

If it is a support programme and the report is on the Total basis, zero is correct — its cost has been allocated onto the mandate programmes. Switch to Direct to see its own cost. Note that saved reports remember their cost basis.

An employee has no cost per hour.

Either no work calendar exists for that budget year — add one under Admin Room → Work Calendars — or the row is a vacant post, which is excluded on purpose.

HR totals look short.

Check Allocation Variances. Salary that has not been allocated to any activity counts in the HR total but appears against no activity or programme.

My changes to a report are not showing.

Refresh with Ctrl+F5. If figures still look stale, confirm the year, entity and cost basis at the top of the report.
